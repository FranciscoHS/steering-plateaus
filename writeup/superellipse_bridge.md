For perturbations along a combination of two directions, this functional form predicts a superellipse relating the breaking angle to the mixing angle. Consider a norm-matched perturbation along $$\hat{v}(\varphi) = \cos\varphi\, d_1 + \sin\varphi\, d_2,$$ with $$d_1 \perp d_2$$, and let $$\theta$$ denote the perturbation angle. Assuming all other features have negligible overlap with $$\hat{v}(\varphi)$$ over the relevant range of $$\varphi$$, the inner sum in the response collapses to two terms: $$R \;=\; F\!\left(\theta^n\bigl[w_1 \cos^n\varphi + w_2 \sin^n\varphi\bigr]\right).$$ The plateau breaks when $$F$$'s argument crosses a fixed level $$\xi^*$$, giving the implicit equation for the breaking angle $$\theta(\varphi)$$: $$\theta(\varphi)^n\bigl[w_1 \cos^n\varphi + w_2 \sin^n\varphi\bigr] \;=\; \xi^*.$$ The single-axis sweeps calibrate the weights at this same threshold: setting $$\varphi = 0$$ gives $$w_1 = \xi^*/\theta_1^n,$$ and $$\varphi = \pi/2$$ gives $$w_2 = \xi^*/\theta_2^n,$$ where $$\theta_1, \theta_2$$ are the measured axis-breaking angles. Substituting these into the implicit equation and dividing through by $$\xi^*$$ eliminates the unknown threshold and yields $$\left(\frac{\theta(\varphi)\cos\varphi}{\theta_1}\right)^{\!n} + \left(\frac{\theta(\varphi)\sin\varphi}{\theta_2}\right)^{\!n} \;=\; 1,$$ a superellipse of exponent $$n$$ in the normalised coordinates $$\bigl(\theta(\varphi)\cos\varphi/\theta_1,\ \theta(\varphi)\sin\varphi/\theta_2\bigr).$$ The right-hand side is $$1$$ rather than a free constant because the two axis measurements absorb $$\xi^*$$ into the normalisers $$\theta_1, \theta_2.$$
